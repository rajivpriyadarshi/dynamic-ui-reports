# Content review — portfolio reports

A content-design pass over every piece of user-facing copy in both reports. Nine findings, each with specific before/after. Mark up anything you disagree with and I'll implement the rest.

---

## 1. The chapter titles don't share a form, and two of them don't answer their own question

Each chapter answers one question for the reader. Right now some titles answer it, some name a document type, and two name something else entirely.

| # | Question the reader has | Prashanth now | Eleanor now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | What's the situation? | The readout | The readout |
| 02 | What's different since last time? | What changed | **Who decides** |
| 03 | How has it done? | What drove performance | **What the record can measure** |
| 04 | What do I own? | Current portfolio | The balance sheet |
| 05 | What's wrong? | What needs attention | What needs attention |
| 06 | What's ahead? | What's coming | What's coming |
| 07 | What do I do? | Decisions for the next conversation | Decisions for the next conversation |

**"Who decides"** is the clearest failure. Chapter 02 asks *what changed*. "Who decides" answers *who has authority* — a different question. A reader scanning the contents has no way to know the chapter contains the four things that moved this period. I retitled it to signal that her changes are all governance rather than market, but that was me labelling my own editorial reasoning instead of telling the reader what's in the section.

**"What the record can measure"** is worse in a different way: it describes my process, not her money. Nobody wants to read about the measuring.

**"The readout"** is jargon borrowed from labs and mission control.

**"Decisions for the next conversation"** is four words doing one word's work.

### Proposed set, used identically in both reports

| # | Proposed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Where things stand | Plain, and it's what the chapter actually says |
| 02 | What changed | Already right. Same title for both; the opening line carries the difference |
| 03 | How the portfolio has done | Works whether the answer is rich (his) or thin (hers) |
| 04 | What he owns / What she owns | Replaces both "Current portfolio" and "The balance sheet" |
| 05 | What needs attention | Already right |
| 06 | What's coming | Already right |
| 07 | What to decide | Same meaning, a third the length |

Five of seven become "What…" questions, so the reader learns the pattern by chapter 03.

**On 02 specifically:** the difference between the two clients belongs in the first sentence, not the title. Hers already opens *"Four things moved since the last review and none of them is a market movement."* That does the job the retitle was trying to do, and it does it in words a reader can follow.

## 2. "Why this matters" appears six times

Used in Prashanth 5A, 5B, 5C, 5E and Eleanor 5A, 5B, 5C.

If a sentence needs a label announcing that it's important, the sentence isn't carrying its own weight. It also implies the surrounding paragraphs *don't* matter.

**Before** (Eleanor 5A):
> **Why this matters.** None of the five is discretionary, so the choice is not whether to pay but what to sell — and the only quick sale is the fixed income that exists to be the reserve.

**After:**
> None of the five is optional, so the question isn't whether to pay — it's what to sell. The only thing she can sell quickly is the bond portfolio that exists to *be* the reserve.

Cut the label everywhere. The paragraph already sits last in its section, which is signal enough.

## 3. Cross-references by number, including sub-numbers

Current instances: *"That is chapter 05"*, *"every percentage in chapter 04"*, *"the governance discussion in 05D would settle"*, *"the liquidity gap in 05A"*, *"the five dated items in 05A"*, *"the technology range in chapter 05"*.

"05D" is documentation-speak. On a page the reader scrolls, numbered cross-references make them hunt. Name the thing instead.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Two of the numbers above are older than they look. That is chapter 05. | Two of the numbers above are older than they look — see *What needs attention*. |
| which is one of the things the governance discussion in 05D would settle | which the frozen governance discussion below would settle |
| the liquidity gap in 05A would ordinarily be closed by | the cash shortfall above would normally be closed by |
| Net worth and every percentage in chapter 04 are computed from these marks | Net worth, and every percentage in *What she owns*, is calculated from these valuations |

## 4. Jargon that has a plain equivalent

These are the ones where the plain word is just as precise:

| Jargon | Plain | Where |
|---|---|---|
| illiquid / not realisable in a quarter | can't be sold quickly | Eleanor 01, 04, 05C, metric label |
| carried at / marks | valued at / valuations | Eleanor throughout |
| cost basis | what she paid | both, ch03 |
| on cost | since purchase | Eleanor ch03 |
| the listed book / the unlisted book | listed holdings / private holdings | Prashanth throughout |
| a derating in one sector | one sector falling | Prashanth 5A |
| synthetic | made up | Prashanth + Eleanor footer |
| readout | — (see finding 1) | both ch01 |

Terms I'd **keep**, because they're the real names and this audience uses them: *look-through*, *uncalled*, *capital call*, *benchmark*, *cost*, *net worth*, *custody*, *drift* (it's defined in place).

## 5. Prashanth's opening sentence is four clauses long

**Before** (40 words, four clauses):
> The portfolio is up 18.4% this year, but most of that came from three technology names — and one of them is now 22.4% of the listed book, spread across 4 custodians so no statement shows it.

Eleanor's equivalent is two clauses and much stronger. His should match.

**After:**
> The portfolio is up 18.4% this year, but three technology names produced most of it — and the largest is spread across four custodians, so no statement shows it whole.

## 6. "Her file" / "his record" is a verbal tic

Eleanor's report says *her file*, *her record*, *her own file*, *the record* fifteen times. It reads defensively, as though the report keeps bracing for a challenge.

Say it once, properly, in *Basis of preparation* — everything here comes from the client file and nothing is estimated — and then delete almost all the rest. Keep it only where the *source* is the actual point, which is the three places where something is missing from the file.

## 7. Metric labels are compressed rather than plain

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Against her floor | Below her minimum |
| Illiquid | Can't be sold quickly |
| Return, 6 months | Return, six months |
| Technology, listed book | Technology holdings |

And the detail line *"S$88.6m not realisable in a quarter"* becomes *"S$88.6m needs longer than three months to sell"*.

## 8. Two places where the document talks about itself

**Before:** *"The chapter is short because the data is."* (Eleanor ch03)
Clever, but it's the report commenting on its own length. The reader doesn't need reassurance about page count.
**After:** delete the sentence. The three preceding sentences already explain what's missing and why.

**Before:** *"Nothing is estimated and nothing is synthetic"* (Eleanor footer)
**After:** *"Nothing here is estimated or made up."*

One meta line I'd **keep**: *"this is the whole schedule, not a selection from it"* (Prashanth 06). That tells the reader nothing is being hidden, which is genuinely useful.

---

## 9. What's already working — don't let a rewrite break it

- **Figure titles state findings, not labels.** *"The things with engines have lost money; the things in cases have not."* *"NVIDIA is one position held four times."* *"Real estate alone is larger than everything that can be sold."* This is the best copy in either report and none of it should change.
- **The 05 subsection headings are full sentences with a verb.** Keep.
- **Numbers are never rounded in prose to flatter.** Keep.
- **Limitations are stated plainly rather than hidden.** Keep the substance; only the jargon needs fixing.

---

## What I'd change, in order of impact

1. The seven chapter titles (finding 1)
2. Delete all six "Why this matters" labels (finding 2)
3. Replace numbered cross-references with names (finding 3)
4. The eight jargon substitutions (finding 4)
5. Trim "her file" from fifteen uses to about four (finding 6)
6. Prashanth's opening sentence, metric labels, the two meta sentences (findings 5, 7, 8)

Nothing here changes a single figure or any layout — it's all copy.
